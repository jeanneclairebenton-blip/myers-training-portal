# Keller Williams vs. Myers: The Investment-Side Competitive Report
**Prepared July 19, 2026 · 106 research agents, 24 sources fetched, 25 top claims adversarially verified (22 confirmed, 3 refuted) · Myers internal numbers pulled live from Zoho CRM**

---

## Bottom line

**KW competes with Myers on the story, not the substance.** As of mid-2026, Keller Williams' entire "agents become investors" offering is (1) paid education and (2) a recruiting-based profit share program. Verified across KW's own pages and 2025–2026 searches: **KW does not fund agent acquisitions, does not buy houses through agents, and does not employ or train agents for acquisition roles.** Every pillar of Myers' actual model — company-funded acquisitions, agents paid to acquire, a defined agent-to-investor ladder, free weekly investor classes — has no KW analog.

The threat from KW is not that they'll do what Myers does. It's that their story is loud enough that agents never go looking for the substance.

---

## What KW is actually doing (verified)

**KW Wealth — their flagship, and it's a paywall.** Launched July 2022, led by top agent Brett Tanner, stated goal of helping agents reach $50M net worth. Three tiers, all confirmed live on KW's site as of July 19, 2026:

| Tier | Price | What you get |
|---|---|---|
| Community | $199/mo (annual commitment) | Weekly live classes: wealth building, investing, tax strategy, money mindset + training library |
| Market Center | $299/mo | Same, at market-center level |
| Mastermind | $1,000/mo | Advanced curriculum + two 2-day live events/year |

That's **$2,388–$12,000/year** for classes. KW teaches agents to find deals and run numbers — **the agent supplies all the capital.** (3-0 verified, KW's own pages.)

**Profit share — big lifetime number, small per-agent reality.** $2.0B+ distributed cumulatively since 1987 (through July 2024). But the run-rate is ~$100M/year across ~174,000 agents = **an average of roughly $567 per agent per year.** And it's extremely concentrated: only 137 associates have ever earned $1M+ lifetime; only ~1.8% have cleared $100K lifetime. It rewards *recruiting tenure*, not investing. KW also tried to cut vested payouts to departed agents in 2023, drew four lawsuits, and partially reversed course — a trust wound worth knowing about when talking to KW agents. (All figures from KW's own press release, 3-0 verified.)

**Corporate structure.** KWx holding company formed October 2020 (including Keller Offers, their iBuying arm at the time). Stone Point Capital took a major stake announced **March 3, 2025** (not 2023 — the research corrected this), size undisclosed, Gary Keller remaining executive chairman. **Important gap:** the current status of Keller Offers, "Keller Capital," and any post-Stone-Point investment strategy produced *zero* claims that survived verification. If KW is building a capital-side program, it isn't publicly visible as of mid-2026 — worth re-checking quarterly.

**Their investor-education IP is a 20-year-old book.** *The Millionaire Real Estate Investor* — published May 2005.

## Where Myers actually stands

| | Myers | Keller Williams | New Western (true analog) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Funds acquisitions with company money | **Yes** | No | Yes (wholesale model) |
| Pays agents to acquire | **Yes** | No | Yes (commission-only) |
| Investor training | **Free weekly live classes** | $199–$1,000/month | In-house, for sourcing |
| Passive/upside income | 10% rev-share pool + 80% commission | Profit share, avg ~$567/agent/yr | None |
| Agent → investor ladder | **Yes — that's the model** | No | No — agents source inventory for resale, forever |

**New Western, not KW, is the structural competitor** — their acquisition agents buy for company inventory using company purchasing power (careers page cites ~$126K average earnings on ~29 transactions/year; self-reported claims of a 250,000-investor marketplace and "a home every 13 minutes" — attributed, unaudited). But New Western is a high-velocity wholesaler: agents source inventory that's resold within days. **They fund the deal; they never make the agent the investor.** Myers is the only model in this comparison where the agent's endpoint is ownership.

**The marketing line this research hands us** (internal framing — never name competitors in published assets, per our standing legal rule):

> *Other brokerages charge you up to $1,000 a month to learn investing. Myers pays you to do it — with our money.*

That sentence is fully defensible: every clause is 3-0 verified.

## Part 2: Measuring Myers' market share in DFW

Two different markets, two different metrics, tracked separately. Plus a bonus third.

### Metric 1 — Share of DFW investor purchases (the acquisitions business)
- **Myers numerator (live from Zoho):** 171 purchases closed FY Jul 2025–Jun 2026 (179 in FY23-24, 151 in FY24-25). ~89% in Dallas (80), Tarrant (41), Collin (21), Denton (14) counties.
- **Denominator:** investor purchases in DFW. The standard methodology is Redfin's: entity-keyword matching on county deed records (LLC/Inc/Trust/Corp buyer names). U.S. investor share was 17% of all home sales in Q3 2025. **Catch: Texas is a non-disclosure state, so Redfin's free 38-metro table has no Texas rows.** One verifier surfaced a possible Dallas figure from Redfin's press materials (18% share, 3,463 investor purchases in Q3 2025) but a second verifier couldn't find the Dallas row — unresolved; check Redfin's downloadable press data directly before relying on it.
- **Preliminary estimate (strategic hypothesis, pending denominator confirmation):** if Dallas-metro investor purchases run ~3,400/quarter → ~13,800/year, Myers' 171 ≈ **~1.2% of DFW investor purchases**. Treat as order-of-magnitude only.
- **Paid upgrade path:** ATTOM's Dallas home-flipping report (quarterly flip counts, flip rate, ROI for DFW) or John Burns; pricing not verified — get quotes.

### Metric 2 — Share of DFW MLS sides (the brokerage/listings business)
- **Denominator:** TRERC (Texas A&M Real Estate Research Center) publishes **free** housing-activity reports for the Dallas–Fort Worth–Arlington MSA, built from NTREIS and 50+ Texas MLSs. Zero cost, no login. Two verified caveats: they count *transactions* not *sides*, and they **explicitly exclude off-MLS sales** — which means this denominator misses exactly the wholesale/off-market deals Myers specializes in. Use it only for the listing side.
- **Myers numerator:** blocked pending Zoho access to the Closed Deals module (sales side). Cleaner still: NTREIS member brokerages can often pull brokerage-level sold-side counts (BrokerMetrics-type products) — ask our broker what NTREIS already gives us free.

### Metric 3 — Agent-headcount share (recruiting scoreboard)
- TREC publishes a **free, daily-updated** dataset of every Texas license holder (~319K records) with sponsoring-broker relationships → we can count agents per brokerage at zero cost.
- Two verified hurdles: the county field is **blank on all records** (verified by live query), so DFW filtering requires mapping specific broker license numbers, and KW franchises are licensed under legal entity names that often don't contain "Keller Williams" (a literal search returned zero). A one-time mapping of DFW KW market-center entities via TREC's License Holder Search solves both.

### The dashboard this becomes
Quarterly, mostly automated: Zoho gives the numerators (I can pull them anytime); TRERC + TREC are free denominators; one ATTOM/John Burns purchase closes the investor-purchase denominator. No MLS access needed to start.
